# Spanish Style Guide — bismliving.com

**Status:** Draft v1. Locked vocabulary + register rules for all Spanish copy on the site. Source: verified against FDLRC, NLACRC, Westside RC, Tri-Counties RC, DDS, Disability Rights California (RULA), and peer SLS/ILS providers.

## Register

- **`usted` / `su` everywhere.** Never `tú` / `tu`, even in CTAs. Every authoritative CA RC/DDS source uses `usted` without exception. The audience is older parents and adult clients; `tú` reads as unprofessional.
- **Neutral Latin American Spanish, light Mexican lean.** No `vosotros`, no `ordenador`, no Spain-isms. No heavy Mexican regionalisms (`platicar`, `chamba`, `ándale`) — alienates Salvadoran/Guatemalan families.
- **US conventions for numbers, dates, phone formats.** `1,000.50` not `1.000,50`.
- **No religious framing.** Despite Catholic-majority client base, no mainstream CA SLS provider leans on faith language. Stay secular-warm.

## Acronyms

**Keep these in English** (community recognizes them): IPP, SLS, ILS, DSP, SDP, DDS, RC. Spell out in Spanish on first use with English acronym in parens, then use the English acronym alone.

> Example: *"Plan del Programa Individual (IPP). El IPP define los servicios que el Centro Regional autoriza."*

**Do NOT coin Spanish acronyms.** Inventing `PPI` for IPP makes families think it's a different program.

## Lead-verb policy

Use **`acompañar`** (to walk alongside) as the primary frame, not `ayudar` (to help). `acompañar` is the single most provider-coded word in this market and what every peer provider uses. Example:

> *"En Bism acompañamos a adultos neurodivergentes…"* (good)
> *"Bism ayuda a adultos neurodivergentes…"* (reads thin / non-provider)

## Tonal anchors (phrases to use)

These are pulled from real provider/RC copy. Echo this style:

- `acompañar` — walk alongside
- `su ser querido` — your loved one (family-facing)
- `en su propia casa` — in their own home (autonomy frame)
- `como una extensión de la familia` — Tri-Counties' phrase
- `a su propio ritmo` — at their own pace (dignity frame)
- `ESCUCHAR – INFORMAR – APOYAR` — Westside RC's triad structure; mirror for hero blocks

## AI-translation tells — DO NOT SHIP

Reject any draft containing any of these. They're the dead giveaways of machine translation in this domain.

1. `Servicios de Vida Apoyada` — wrong. Use `Servicios de Vida con Apoyo`. **#1 tell.**
2. `vendedor` — means *salesperson*. Use `proveedor`.
3. `consumidor` — calque. Use `cliente` or `persona que recibe los servicios`.
4. `discapacidad de desarrollo` — missing article. Always `del desarrollo`.
5. `Plan Individual del Programa` — wrong word order. Use `Plan del Programa Individual`.
6. `cuidador directo` — wrong. Use `Profesional de Apoyo Directo`.
7. `informado en/por trauma` — clumsy. Use `atención basada en el trauma`.
8. Mixed `tú` and `usted` on the same page.
9. `Centro de la Región` for Regional Center — wrong. Use `Centro Regional`.
10. English-style title case in headings (`Servicios De Vida Con Apoyo`). Spanish capitalizes only the first word + proper nouns.
11. Em dashes everywhere (`—`). AI overuses; Spanish prefers parens, colons, periods.
12. `centrado en personas` (plural) instead of `centrado en la persona` (singular + article).

## Typography & formatting

- Headings: sentence case only (first word + proper nouns capitalized).
- Inverted opening punctuation: `¿` and `¡` required.
- `ñ` and accented characters must be intact — verify in CMS, never copy from PDF without re-checking diacritics.
- Apostrophes: curly quotes `"…"` or `«…»` are equivalent in modern usage; pick one and stay consistent.

## Native review

Before any Spanish copy ships:

1. Grep the file against every "AI tell" above. Zero matches.
2. Have a Mexican-American or Salvadoran-American native speaker (not a professional translator — they over-formalize) read it as a parent looking for SLS for their adult son. Brief: *"Flag anything that sounds like Google Translate or that you wouldn't say."*
